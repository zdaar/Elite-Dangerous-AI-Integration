# Field guide — finding and identifying organisms

Technique for the part of the loop that happens after you've arrived. Sources: Canonn's Organic Scanning Guide, plus 2025–2026 hands-on video guides.

## The value heuristic you can use from the cockpit

**High payers are large, sculptural, and solitary. Low payers are small, dense, ground-hugging mats or grass.**

That single rule gets you most of the way. Tubus towers and Osseus skeletons are worth millions each; the grass-like Tussock carpets and Bacterium stains mostly aren't (with a few notable exceptions — Tussock Stigmasis is 19M).

## Arrival gate — controller-first, exact body only

The legacy-candidate route already provides a **system and exact body designation**. Do not turn it into a system survey.

1. Jump, honk, and try to select the named body directly from the navigation panel or system map.
2. If it is unresolved, open FSS, tune directly to the high-metal-content band, and resolve **only the named body** (or only the short list of named HMC candidates supplied for that system). Stop as soon as the target is identified. Never complete the FSS to 100% for this money loop.
3. Read the BioInsights prediction immediately after the target resolves. Reject the body before supercruise when it does not predict a species above the configured value floor.
4. Treat biological-signal count only as context. The claim that a one-signal HMC is usually Bacterium is an anecdotal shortcut, not a rule; one signal can be the valuable organism being hunted. **BioInsights overrides signal-count guessing.**
5. Check the in-game First Footfall state. An existing name lowers the expected First Logged upside, but no name is still only a clue—not proof that the 5x record remains available.
6. Fly out and DSS **only after** the body passes the prediction/value gate.
7. For the Tectonicas lane, require a **`Stratum` DSS filter**. If mapping does not expose that filter, leave immediately. BioInsights predicts after FSS and validates after DSS; the DSS result is the final go/no-go gate.

The DSS overlay shows compatible terrain, not organism density. Do not interpret brighter, darker, solid, or pixelated blue as “more specimens.” Toggle the overlay off when necessary to read the underlying terrain, and prefer places where valuable species' compatible areas overlap.

If the named bodies have already been resolved or the system is already at 100% FSS, use those results. Never ask the commander to repeat or complete FSS as a ritual. A question about landing or surface identification stays on the current body and must not advance the expedition queue.

## Approach and landing

Use a small, maneuverable ship and come in at **30–50°** for a fast glide. Deploy the cargo scoop as an airbrake if useful. For Stratum Tectonicas, use open, flat compatible terrain: make an initial broad pass around **400 m**, then descend below **100 m** and slow down for visual confirmation and landing.

Prioritise the genus with the *smallest* mapped area first — it will usually overlap a larger one, so you can bank multiple genera without extra take-offs.

## On the surface

**The Artemis Organic Sampler pulse reaches 750 m**, increased from 75 m on 29 Sep 2025. Most older guides assume the old figure, so sweep far more broadly than they recommend.

The alt-fire pulse colour-codes what it lights up:

- **Green** — far enough from your previous sample of this species; safe to scan
- **Blue** — still inside the colony radius; move further
- **Purple** — a *different* species. Sampling it discards your in-progress set

That purple warning exists precisely because the discard rule is live. It's the game telling you not to.

**Composition Scanner pings audibly** when swept across a surface organism. On dark or far-from-star worlds, sit in the SRV turret in Analysis mode with **lights and night vision off** and sweep — this is the only reliable way to find Bacterium, which doesn't render under night vision and is often partly sub-surface.

On icy bodies, fly *toward* the sun's reflection: organisms reflect less than ice and darken into visibility against the glare.

Finish all three samples of the current organism before touching another one. Switching species discards the incomplete set. A Composition Scan is useful for Codex/variant records, but it is not a required detour for the Vista money loop.

## Moving between samples

For flat Stratum terrain, stay under 100 m, nose down, and use vertical thrusters to hold altitude. Re-landing beside each colony is generally faster than driving an SRV.

For **Stratum Tectonicas**, move at least **500 m** from the previous sample before scanning again. Use the sampler pulse instead of estimating: green means the next sample is valid; blue means keep moving.

**The external camera trick:** deploy the camera and pull back for a pseudo-drone view to spot the next specimen instead of searching blind. Sling it under the front landing leg near ground level while the ship stays high — ground-level visibility with no collision risk. Fly higher for Bacterium, lower for Tussock and Frutexa.

**Optional Nomad:** if owned, use its cockpit-operated Mk II Biological Scanner and small landing footprint to shorten the surface search. Frontier has not published a numeric Mk II scanner range, so do not assume or promise one. It does not replace DSS mapping, disembarking in an Artemis suit, or collecting three valid samples.

## Colour variants

Determined by parent star class (roughly 70% of species) or by crustal material (the rest). The star-class → colour mapping is constant within a genus; the material → colour mapping differs per species.

**Colour does not affect credit value.** Vista Genomics prices per species, with no colour dimension. Anemones, Brain Trees and Sinuous Tubers look like exceptions but aren't — there the colour *is* the species name, and all colours are priced identically.

Variants matter only for Codex completion and the first-logged-in-region flags. If you're chasing Codex rather than credits, that changes which systems you want — but it doesn't change what a body pays.

Full star-class → colour tables live in EDMC-ExploData's `genus.py`; see `elite-local-data`.

## Don't die

Unsold biodata is lost on death with no insurance. On a long deep-space run you may be carrying several hundred million credits of unsold data. Sell before anything risky, and be aware that a botched landing on a high-gravity body is the most common way commanders lose an entire expedition.
